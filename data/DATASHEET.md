# Datasheet: Synthetic Hallmark-Inscription Dataset (v1)

**Provenance: 100 % SYNTHETIC.** Every image is a procedural rendering produced by `src/hallmark/synth.py`. None is a photograph of real jewellery, and none should be described as such. Licence: CC0-1.0 (all content generated by this project's code). The `provenance` column of `manifest.csv` is `synthetic` for every row.

## Composition
| split | items | images | readable | partial | unreadable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 144 | 864 | 476 | 221 | 167 |
| val | 48 | 288 | 158 | 68 | 62 |
| test | 48 | 288 | 152 | 79 | 57 |
| total | 240 | 1440 | 786 | 368 | 286 |

* 240 fictitious inscriptions ("items"), 80 for each of three rendering "sources" S1-S3 (different base-metal colour/texture and font sets, used for the leave-one-source-out test).
* 6 acquisitions per item (condition-dependent degradation draws), 640x400 px.
* Styles: engraved, embossed, stamped, laser. Conditions: clean, blur, lowres, uneven_light, glare, lowcontrast, angle, occlusion, noise, combined, severe.
* **Split is by item**: all acquisitions of one inscription are in one split (asserted in `scripts/01_make_dataset.py`), so no inscription text/rendering leaks between train and test. Note that different items can still share *common tokens* (e.g. "916", "BIS") by design of the grammar.

## Text grammar (fictitious)
Tokens are drawn from purity-style numerals, karat labels, "BIS", a random 6-character code and a 2-3 letter maker mark, shuffled, joined by spaces and **cut at 16 characters**. The cut can truncate the last token (about 10 % of items, e.g. "KSMFD2 BIS 375 K"), so a grammar-validity feature flags some correct transcriptions as invalid. This is a known generator property; the grammar feature is used only in an ablation (S6), not in the headline model. The strings are labels for OCR evaluation and **carry no meaning about gold purity or authenticity**.

## Labels
* `text`: ground-truth transcription. `bbox`: ground-truth text box.
* Per-factor quality labels `q_blur, q_glare, q_resolution, q_contrast, q_lighting, q_occlusion, q_viewing` are **thresholds on the generator's own degradation parameters**.
* `readability` (readable / partial / unreadable) is a **generator-defined proxy** (a function of those factor labels), *not* a human legibility judgement. Classifier accuracy therefore measures recovery of this proxy, not human perception.
* OCR correctness (the target of the rejector) is measured against `text`, whitespace-insensitive.

## Known limitations
No real metal, no real lighting physics, no real hallmark die geometry or Indian/other national hallmark rules. Rendered glare, relief and perspective are simplified models. Results here say nothing about performance on photographs of real jewellery.

## Reproduce
`python3 scripts/01_make_dataset.py` (seed in `configs/default.json`).

## Bundled sample (`data/sample/`)
27 SYNTHETIC test-split images (3 per source x readability class: readable / partial / unreadable) with `manifest_sample.csv` (same columns as `manifest.csv`). Included so the repository runs without regenerating the full 1,440-image set (about 537 MB, not bundled; `python3 scripts/01_make_dataset.py` recreates it from the fixed seeds). All images are procedurally generated; none shows real jewellery.
